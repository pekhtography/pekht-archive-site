Every city rewrites itself in steel and glass. But the river keeps the original draft — orange rust, slow weight, the smell of moving earth.

The bridge spans the gap it created. Water below carries every shadow that ever crossed it. Permanence is just a longer kind of passing.

White ribs of towers stare at the water line. The electric prow cuts the reflection — jagged concrete smiles crumble into waves. Heavy architecture thinks it owns the river. The vessel disagrees.

The old world roars on the shore; the new one glides silent across the river. One leaves a wake of noise, the other — a shimmer of pure light.

Water ruled here first. Glass arrived later. Now the river just keeps rolling. The white tram nudges past — a small, wet laugh against all that vertical glass. Towers watch. Grass grows. People walk. Eternity smells like damp leaves and moves on.
